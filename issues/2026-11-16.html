The Lead

The product graph arms race

Reuters reports that Amazon is considering a “World Product Graph,” a catalog of virtually every item for sale, including products it does not stock. The project is described in an internal PR/FAQ, so it is a reported plan rather than a launched product; Amazon says the document is outdated and does not necessarily reflect current plans. Its logic, though, is dead serious. An agent needs canonical facts: which model is current, whether two listings describe the same item, and what constraints actually apply. Amazon’s document says its own shopping answers can be wrong or incomplete, and frames better data as a way to improve accuracy and use cheaper models. That makes the contest less about a charming shopping chatbot than about who owns the factual substrate beneath it. Why it matters: for agentic commerce, clean product truth is the interface before the interface.
THE TAKEAWAY
Distribution once meant owning the storefront. With agents in the middle, the durable advantage may be owning the structured facts, identifiers and availability rules that determine whether the recommendation can be trusted.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Entity resolution: the unglamorous product moat
Intuition: entity resolution is the work of deciding whether two records refer to the same real thing. It is why “AirPods Pro, white, 2nd gen” from three sellers should become one product with three offers—not three near-misses. Think of it as an immigration desk for data: every claim needs an identity, evidence and a decision about whether it belongs.

Mechanism:
merchant feeds → identifiers and conflict rules → canonical product → reliable agent answer
Application: Amazon’s reported graph matters because models do not solve this by sounding confident. A robust system preserves the source, confidence and freshness of each attribute; it also knows when two values conflict. Try this (20 min): choose one product in your own backlog. Make a tiny sheet with five raw listings, a canonical identifier, source URLs, price, availability and last-checked time. Highlight every field that could disagree. You have just found the place where an agent needs policy, not prose.
⚽ The Weekend in Football
Manchester United have appointed Mauricio Pochettino after Michael Carrick’s dismissal. There was no Premier League round this weekend because of the international window, so the story is not a scoreline—it is the reset. United’s previous league match was a wild 4–4 draw with Aston Villa: enough attacking life to avoid embarrassment, too little control to resemble a team with a repeatable plan. That is football’s version of incident design: when the system fails, does everyone revert to improvisation, or do the constraints still hold?
