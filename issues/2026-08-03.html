The Lead
Trump calls off Iran's biggest strike of the war, opens Hormuz talks today
Trump said Sunday he'd personally approved cancelling what officials had called potentially the largest US military operation since World War II — a weekend strike on Iran's power grid and refineries — after Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Iran all asked for more time to negotiate. New US-Iran talks over reopening the Strait of Hormuz begin today, with American forces staying on full alert in case they collapse. The reversal followed a bloody stretch on both sides: three family members killed in US strikes on Qeshm Island, four dead in an Iranian missile strike on Haifa. Brent crude, which had gained 24% in July pricing in the escalation, dropped roughly 5% within hours as that premium unwound.

Why it matters: five months of war just pivoted on a phone call, not a battlefield outcome — proof this conflict was always more negotiable than it looked, whenever either side wanted it to be.
The Takeaway
A war that spent five months escalating toward “the largest strike since World War II” just stood down over a negotiation nobody had announced was close — that's not caution, it's Washington, Tehran, Riyadh and Abu Dhabi all deciding the risk of proceeding outweighed the risk of stopping. Whether today's Hormuz talks produce anything durable, or just another pause before the next provocation restarts the clock, is the only question that's mattered here since February.

🤖 AI & Tech
DeepSeek goes rogue via a Telegram-controlled hacking bot, Onyx raises $113M to police AI agents, and an unreleased OpenAI model solves 10 open math problems
A Chinese-speaking hacker wired DeepSeek into an open-source agent framework to run cyberattacks with zero human steering: Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 traced threat actor “knaithe,” who directed DeepSeek via Telegram inside the open-source Hermes Agent to enumerate targets, source exploits and breach internet-facing servers — confirming compromises via a Citrix NetScaler flaw and 11 exposed notebook instances. Caught only because the bot's own web server leaked its operator's logs.

Onyx Security raised $113 million to build a “Guardian Agent” that polices other AI agents, four months after its Series A. Its control plane already supervises 1.1 million agents and 1.8 million employees across customers, with the Bessemer-led round pricing it near $640 million. The pitch: as agents get wired deeper into the enterprise stack, someone needs a supervisory layer watching them in real time — timely, given the item above.

An unreleased OpenAI model called Astra solved 10 long-open math and computer-science problems, publishing formal, machine-checkable Lean proofs on GitHub for roughly $2,000 in compute — including a non-sofic group construction open since 1999. Fields medalist Timothy Gowers said he'd recommend one of the proofs for a top journal without hesitation — a capability signal ahead of whatever OpenAI names its next flagship.

Brussels started enforcing new AI Act transparency rules on August 2: chatbots must now disclose they're AI, deepfakes need labelling, and AI-generated content requires machine-readable marks. Anyone shipping conversational or generative features into the EU just had disclosure UX turn from a nice-to-have into a compliance requirement.

Simile raised $200 million to simulate all 8 billion people on Earth, hitting a $2 billion valuation just five months after its Series A. The synthetic-user startup builds behavioural simulation models enterprises use to pressure-test pricing, product and campaign decisions before shipping them to real customers — run the customer research before you burn the customer.
🌏 World & Malaysia
Gaza's own peace mediator turns on Israel, an Indonesian ferry fire leaves 41 missing, and Negeri Sembilan swears in a new government
Gaza's own peace mediator accused Israel of undermining the ceasefire framework it just helped write: Board of Peace director-general Nickolay Mladenov said two days of Israeli strikes across Gaza “killed civilians and destroyed medical supplies,” warning it undermines the Egypt-Turkey-Qatar-US-mediated roadmap Trump announced days earlier — even as Netanyahu's office still hasn't signed on, as covered Saturday.

Russia fired 133 drones at Ukraine overnight, and Kyiv struck back at a Russian air base and refinery: combined strikes across eight Ukrainian regions and two Russian sites killed at least 14 on both sides — the war grinding on even as Friday's Poland airspace breach hasn't recurred.

An Indonesian ferry fire off Madura island has left 41 people missing: the Mutiara Sentosa 2, carrying 271 people from Surabaya to Makassar, caught fire Sunday morning; five are confirmed dead and 225 rescued so far, with the cause still unknown.

Barisan Nasional swept Negeri Sembilan's snap election, ending Pakatan Harapan's state government: BN and ally PN combined for 25 of 36 seats — a two-thirds supermajority, not the hung assembly forecasters expected, as covered Saturday — and caretaker Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun lost his own seat by nearly 3,000 votes. BN's Ismail Lasim was sworn in as MB on Sunday.

Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof will undergo heart bypass surgery: his office confirmed the procedure Monday on medical advice, describing him as stable and asking the public to keep him in their prayers, with no date given for his return to duties.

Najib Razak faces a RM51.4 billion civil suit as the 1MDB trial finally opens: 1MDB's lawyers told the High Court the former PM was “at the centre” of the fraud alongside fugitive financier Jho Low, seeking to recover the state fund's claimed losses.
📈 Markets

US futures point sharply higher this morning after Sunday's Iran de-escalation, building on Friday's close of S&P 500 7,489.72 (+0.7%) and Nasdaq Composite 25,373.85 (+1.0%) — S&P 500 futures are up roughly 0.6–1.0% and Nasdaq 100 futures 0.6–1.6%.
