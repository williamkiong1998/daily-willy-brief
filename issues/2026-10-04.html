☕ Cold Open
AI’s bottleneck is moving from “which model?” to “who can make it dependable inside a messy institution?” This morning’s thread is the same across an engineer residency, a data-centre town hall and a WhatsApp appeal: capability earns its keep only when a real system can absorb it.
The Lead

The deployment skill gets trained

Anthropic is committing $100 million to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. The new residency starts with a multi-day programme, then puts nominated engineers through a 12-week real enterprise deployment before a graded practical and credential. Initial cohorts include consultancies and enterprises such as Accenture, Bain, Deloitte and Morgan Stanley. This is an announcement, not evidence that the model will work; but its design is revealing. The scarce asset is no longer access to a capable model. It is the person who can turn a use case into a governed workflow, observe failure and hand it back to an operating team. Why it matters: organisations that treat AI fluency as a course will trail organisations that treat it as supervised production work.
THE TAKEAWAY
Every AI roadmap has a people-shaped dependency hiding behind the model name. The winning implementation team will look less like a prompt-help desk and more like a clinical rotation: real cases, bounded authority, inspection, then a wider licence to operate.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Scenario-based authority
Intuition: a certification should resemble a driving test, not a lecture theatre. Knowledge matters, but the moment that counts is whether someone can make a safe judgement when the road is wet, another driver behaves oddly and the map is incomplete.

Mechanism:
use case → bounded scenario → observed execution → reviewable trace → wider authority
The Anthropic residency’s useful idea is not the badge. It is that authority follows evidence: a participant works on a named deployment, then earns a credential after an assessed practical. That creates feedback at the exact point where policy meets product.

Application: Try this (20 min): choose one workflow you would let an agent assist with. Invent one awkward case—conflicting customer data, an unapproved recipient or a failed tool. Write the expected escalation, the trace you would inspect and the single person allowed to resolve it. You have the beginnings of a practical exam, and more importantly, a safer operating contract.
