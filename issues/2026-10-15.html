The Lead

The AI boom just got a higher hurdle rate

Hot U.S. inflation made the market reprice AI infrastructure in a single session. September CPI rose 3.7% year over year, above August’s 3.4%, while the S&P 500 fell 1.1% and the Nasdaq 1.9%. The immediate hit landed on companies that turn future compute demand into present capital expenditure: CoreWeave fell 15.5%, Oracle 8.8%, while Nvidia, Broadcom and AMD also declined. That is not a referendum on whether models will be used. It is a reminder that servers, power and land are financed long before their utilisation proves out. Why it matters: when the discount rate rises, the winning AI product is not merely the cleverest one; it is the one whose demand arrives quickly enough to carry the infrastructure behind it.
The Takeaway
Higher rates do not kill AI investment; they separate capacity from capability. Builders now need an explicit chain from workload → revenue → utilisation → funding, because a breathtaking model demo cannot service construction debt.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Unit economics under a higher hurdle rate
Intuition: a hurdle rate is the minimum return an investment needs before it deserves capital. Think of it as a high-jump bar: the same athlete may be impressive, but the bar has moved. For an AI feature, the question is not “can it work?”; it is “can the value arrive soon and reliably enough to justify the model, workflow and support costs?”

Mechanism:
customer workflow → measurable value → utilisation and margin → funding confidence → capacity decision
Application: yesterday’s sell-off matters because data centres are a leveraged version of this chain. If financing costs rise before utilisation does, the gap is expensive. Product teams face the smaller but identical problem: an agent that saves ten minutes only creates value when it is used, trusted and priced appropriately. Try this (20 min): choose one AI feature and write a five-line investment case: user frequency, time or revenue saved per use, model cost per use, adoption assumption, and the date the feature pays back its build cost. If you cannot name the measurement event for each line, you have a demo—not yet a business case.
🔧 Product Craft
Generated UI should be disposable, not authoritative
OpenAI’s Intelligent UI suggests a useful distinction for product builders: let the model arrange the room, but do not let it move the walls. A generated comparison table, temporary research view or calculation tool can be excellent precisely because it is local to a task. The danger begins when that temporary surface quietly becomes the system of record, hides the source data or creates an irreversible action. Design the interface contract first: which components are allowed, which data fields are authoritative, what needs confirmation, and how a user can fall back to a plain, inspectable view. The more adaptive the presentation, the more stable those rails should be.
