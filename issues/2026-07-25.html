The Lead
Iran struck back at two American bases — and Trump is weighing the biggest offensive of the war
As covered through the week, the US has struck Iran nightly since the war reignited — Friday marked the 13th consecutive night of American strikes, with Iranian state media claiming four dead, unverified. Iran answered by hitting US bases in Bahrain and Jordan with drones, and Trump told advisers he's close to authorising what he called an attack “bigger than anything so far.” One senior Arab official summed up the regional mood to NBC: “It's only getting crazier.” Yet WTI crude eased 2.25% to $90.12 on Friday, pulling back from Thursday's break above $100 — markets betting on de-escalation nobody in Washington is actually signalling.

Why it matters: a war that just widened to two more American bases and a threatened “biggest offensive yet” is a mispriced risk if oil is calming down while the fighting isn't.
The Takeaway
Iran hitting two American bases and Trump floating the war's biggest offensive yet is the sharpest escalation since the conflict reopened — but oil eased instead of spiking. Markets have spent two weeks re-pricing every Houthi tanker strike; a direct US-Iran clash at the scale now being discussed hasn't been tested yet, and that gap is complacency, not calm.

🤖 AI & Tech
Stripe circles a $10B model marketplace, AMD bets $5B on Anthropic, and Moonshot AI eyes a $50B Hong Kong listing
Stripe is in talks to buy OpenRouter for close to $10 billion: the AI-model marketplace, valued at $1.3 billion in its last round, gives 5 million-plus developers one interface to compare and switch between OpenAI, Anthropic and open-weight models. Stripe already handles its payments — owning the routing layer lets it steer enterprise customers to whichever model is cheapest for the job.

AMD is putting up to $5 billion into Anthropic to lock in 2 gigawatts of chip orders: as covered Thursday when AMD unveiled its Helios rack, this is the demand-side half of that bet — Anthropic deploys AMD's MI450 GPUs for Claude from H1 2027, with AMD's investment released as deployment milestones hit. Equity for guaranteed tens of billions in server orders.

DeepSeek's V4 pricing just stopped being flat: the now-stable release introduces peak/off-peak surge pricing, doubling V4-Pro's output cost during Beijing business hours ($0.87 to $1.74 per million tokens) while undercutting everyone off-peak. Cost models built on flat API pricing just inherited a timezone problem.

Moonshot AI is chasing a $50 billion valuation on the way to a Hong Kong IPO: Kimi K3, its 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model, pushed annual recurring revenue from $200 million in April to $300 million in June — demand strong enough that Moonshot paused new consumer subscriptions days after launch. Goldman and CICC are circling as underwriters.

Block open-sourced a workspace where AI agents carry their own cryptographic ID: Buzz, built on the Nostr protocol, gives every agent a keypair tied back to a human owner via a second signature — an audit trail as agents post, review code and ship work like teammates. Block is already running it internally instead of Slack and GitHub.
🌏 World & Malaysia
Netanyahu's next challenger wears a uniform, Zelensky braces for a bigger strike, and Malaysia gets hit with a fresh US tariff
Israel keeps bombing Gaza despite the “ceasefire”: strikes Thursday killed at least two Palestinians in areas where residents had just been ordered to flee. Al Jazeera puts the toll at 1,180 Palestinians killed since the US-brokered deal was signed last October — a truce in name more than practice.

Zelensky says Russia could launch a massive missile strike within 48 hours: the Friday warning came alongside intelligence pointing to a fall mobilisation wave, as Ukrainian drones hit warehouses and energy sites in Belgorod and set fires at Wildberries facilities in St Petersburg — Russia's answer to Amazon, now a repeat target.

Netanyahu's next fight isn't Hamas — it's a former general: after three years of war and mounting public frustration, a retired military chief is emerging as a serious challenger ahead of Israel's autumn political reckoning, Israel's longest-serving PM now facing a threat from inside the security establishment.

Malaysia gets hit with a new 10% US tariff: Trump's Section 301 order, effective Friday, applies the levy to 60 trading partners — Malaysia among them — over what Washington calls inadequate enforcement against forced-labour imports; the 38 countries on the harsher tier face 12.5%.

The FBM KLCI just logged its fourth losing session in five: down 0.79% to 1,701.02 Friday, off 1.76% for the week, as oil above $100 and the new tariff news kept traders defensive. Energy counters were the lone bright spot.

Early voting opens Tuesday in Negeri Sembilan: 889,490 voters are eligible ahead of the August 1 polling day, with 21 of 36 seats already shaping up as three-way fights between the new BN-PN pact and Anwar's unity bloc.
📈 Markets
Intel blew past every estimate and still dropped 11% — the third earnings beat this week Wall Street punished anyway
The S&P 500 closed Friday essentially flat at 7,411.98 (+0.05%), while the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.64% to 24,975.82 as chip stocks dragged; the Dow outperformed, up 0.46% to 51,947.25 on a 3.5% jump in Apple. Intel was the story: revenue of $16.1 billion, up 25% and $1.7 billion ahead of estimates, with its data-centre and AI unit up 59% — yet shares fell 11%, erasing roughly $90 billion in market cap, after Intel raised 2026 capex guidance past $20 billion and warned 2027 spending goes higher still. It's the third straight earnings-day sell-the-beat reaction this week, following Alphabet (Wednesday) and Tesla (Thursday). Sharper number: Apple briefly overtook Nvidia as the world's most valuable company intraday before Nvidia reclaimed the lead, $4.92 trillion to $4.89 trillion, by the close.

What to expect: the Fed meets July 28–29, decision Wednesday 2pm ET (a hold is priced at roughly 89%); Q2 GDP and the PCE inflation print land Thursday July 30, the same morning Amazon is expected to report.
