☕ Cold Open

The old web funnel was built to get a person to a page. The new one is trying to keep them in a useful conversation long enough to make a considered choice. That sounds like advertising, but it is really an interface problem: where does persuasion end, product truth begin, and a human remain in control?

The Lead

The ad is becoming a conversation

OpenAI is testing Sponsored Agents: a clearly labelled, business-sponsored conversation a person may enter after clicking an ad in ChatGPT. The company says the agent is separate from ChatGPT’s independent answers and from the original conversation; it is initially being tested with select US advertisers. Alongside it, OpenAI is adding natural-language campaign management, creative assistance and integrations with HubSpot and Shopify. This is a company product announcement, not proof that the format improves outcomes. But the structural shift is real: an ad no longer has to terminate in a landing page. It can become an accountable handoff into product information, fit questions and the moment a buyer decides whether to leave the platform.

Why it matters: the next conversion advantage may belong less to the cleverest creative than to the brand that can answer a real question without hiding the boundary between help, sales and independent advice.
The Takeaway
Conversational commerce collapses discovery, explanation and intent into one surface. The durable product will make provenance and exits visible: who is speaking, what they can do, which facts they know, and when the customer has crossed from exploration into a transaction.
🤖 AI & Tech
Agents are becoming a product surface, not a sidecar
01
OpenAI’s Agents API packages the long-running harness—context, tools, environments and subagents—behind a managed API. Customer examples are vendor-supplied claims, but they point to a pragmatic change: buying an agent runtime means buying its failure modes too. Keep the workflow’s business state, permissions and UX legible even when the execution harness is outsourced.
02
Meta’s Muse puts a personal agent in a dedicated virtual machine and asks people to choose what each connected app may do. Its announced model includes approval before consequential actions such as sending email or purchasing. The design lesson is sharper than the launch claim: delegated action needs a visible access gradient, not a single, irreversible “connect” button.
03
Google is moving legacy Dynamic Search Ads into AI Max, while retaining brand, location and text controls. The migration began this month for some settings; the full DSA sunset was deferred to February 2027. Automation becomes commercially safe when operators can preserve the constraints that made the old system acceptable.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Intent handoffs: move context without smuggling influence
Intuition: an intent handoff transfers a person from one system to another while preserving the reason they arrived, the information they have seen and their remaining choices. Think of a hotel concierge: the handoff is helpful when they know you need a late check-in; it becomes creepy when they quietly decide which room you ought to book.

Mechanism:
stated need → disclosed sponsor and scope → bounded context transfer → user choice → logged outcome
Sponsored Agents make the boundary visible: they are labelled, separate from independent answers, and linked outward when the person is ready. A strong handoff carries only the minimum useful context, declares which party benefits, and offers a reversible exit. Otherwise “personalisation” is just a euphemism for hidden steering.

Try this (20 min): map one conversion flow in your product. At the handoff, write four fields: intent captured, data transferred, new actor’s authority and the exit route. If any field is implicit, add it to the interface or event log before adding more automation.
🔧 Product Craft
Treat the agent handoff as a designed moment
Sponsored Agents are useful as a product pattern because they make the new speaker explicit before the conversation deepens. That is the part worth stealing. A handoff should answer: who am I now talking to, why am I here, what will this party do with my context, and how do I leave? Put those answers before the first high-friction request, not inside a buried privacy link. Then design the conversation around decisions, not maximum engagement: compare options, surface constraints, link to source material and pause before money or commitment. The best agentic interface feels less like being funnelled down a corridor and more like a good shop assistant pointing to the aisle, then giving you room to think.
