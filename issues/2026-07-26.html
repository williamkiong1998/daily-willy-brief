The Lead
The US didn't strike Iran overnight — the first pause in 13 nights, as Oman pushes a Hormuz deal
As covered Saturday, when Trump was weighing the war's “biggest offensive yet,” that decision never came. Instead, Omani officials flew to Tehran on Friday for direct talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and US Central Command didn't announce a single strike overnight — the first quiet night after 13 straight. Trump said he'd made no decision on Thursday's threatened massive attack, telling reporters “I think they're being serious,” while warning the US remains “locked and loaded.” Iran's health ministry counts 59 dead and 666 injured since June 27. Oil, which broke $100 Thursday, eased to $96.88 by Friday's close.

Why it matters: a pause built on live negotiations isn't a ceasefire — “locked and loaded” means this is one stalled talk away from resuming.
The Takeaway
The most dovish line of this five-month war — Trump saying Iran is “being serious” — arrived bundled with a warning that the US is still locked and loaded. Read it as diplomacy buying time, not a resolution: oil and equities are pricing in a pause, not a deal, and that gap is exactly where the next escalation would catch everyone off guard.

🤖 AI & Tech
Jensen Huang finally tweets, GPT-5.6 tries to out-code Claude, and a viral coding assistant becomes a supply-chain warning
Jensen Huang used his first-ever X post to defend open AI models: signed alongside Microsoft, Meta and Hugging Face, the letter warns Washington against repeating software's 1980s lock-down mistake by restricting open-weight models — a pointed rebuttal to advisers accusing Moonshot's Kimi K3 of being a distilled copy of a US model. Nvidia sells chips regardless of whose model runs on them.

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family ships with a flagship built to out-code Claude: three tiers — Luna, Terra and Sol — priced from $1 to $30 per million tokens, with Sol scoring 80 on the Artificial Analysis coding index, 2.8 points above Fable 5. A fourth serious frontier-coding release this month alone.

Alibaba's Agent Native Cloud puts a number on agent ROI: unveiled at WAIC, AgentTeams and AgentLoop orchestrate and audit multi-agent workflows; internally, 15 coordinated agents now handle 85% of Alibaba's own developer-support tickets. Every agent vendor promises this — here's one with receipts.

GitHub's most-starred AI coding project is also a supply-chain case study: OpenClaw beat React's decade-old star record in 60 days, past 250,000-plus — then its skill marketplace got hit by “ClawHavoc,” a campaign planting malware in hundreds of unreviewed plugins. Popularity and provenance aren't the same thing.

Etched just proved the chip market still has room for a bet against Nvidia: the inference-specialised startup closed a $300 million Series C at a $10.3 billion valuation, Sequoia-led with SK Hynix and Jane Street joining. Investors still buy the thesis that transformer-specific silicon beats general-purpose GPUs.
🌏 World & Malaysia
Kyiv takes its deadliest hit in weeks, Ladakh's hunger striker stands down, and Putrajaya picks a fight over “Tanah Melayu”
A Russian missile hit a Kyiv arms exhibition, killing 10: the Friday strike injured around 100 more, as Ukrainian forces answered by hitting a Tyumen refinery over 2,000km inside Russia — the drone war now reaches deep on both sides.

Gaza's ceasefire has existed on paper for 284 straight days: Israeli strikes and gunfire continue daily despite October's truce, with 1,100-plus Palestinians killed since it was signed. A new campaign, “They Lied to You,” pushes back on the world's read that the war ended.

An Indian hunger striker forced a government climbdown after 26 days: Sonam Wangchuk ended his fast once ministers promised action on exam mismanagement, but the “Cockroach” protest movement he sparked still wants the education minister gone.

Anwar had to publicly slap down his own coalition's ally: PN's Muhammad Sanusi told a Kedah ceramah that non-Malays have “ancestral homelands” to fall back on; MCA and MIC — usually BN's allies, not PN's — piled on before Anwar called it an insult to loyal citizens.

Malaysia could get an F1 race back on the calendar within months: Anwar says today's announcement covers Sepang potentially stepping in for October's Bahrain Grand Prix — at risk from the same Iran war dominating this newsletter.

The ringgit is holding near RM4.08–4.10, waiting on two events: next week's Fed decision and August 1's Negeri Sembilan polls, even as June trade data ran hot — exports up 45.4% year-on-year to RM177.9 billion.
📈 Markets
Digital Realty got rewarded for the same AI capex bill that just sank Alphabet, Tesla and Intel
Markets were closed for the weekend, so Friday's levels stand: the S&P 500 closed at 7,411.98 (+0.05%), and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.64% to 24,975.82. Digital Realty was the mover: shares jumped 13% after Q2 revenue rose 29% year-on-year to $1.9 billion and 2026 guidance was lifted to $6.85–6.95 billion — a data-centre landlord rewarded for the exact AI-capex build-out that punished Alphabet, Tesla and Intel on their own earnings days this week. Sharper number: the semiconductor gauge sank 4.3% Friday even as the S&P barely moved — AI anxiety concentrated in chipmakers, not the broader tape.

What to expect: the Fed delivers its rate decision Wednesday, July 29, 2pm ET — the first live test of Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish June dot plot, where 9 of 18 officials penciled in a 2026 hike; Apple and Microsoft both report Thursday, July 30.
