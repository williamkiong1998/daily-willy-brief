☕ Cold Open

The expensive part of AI is becoming less obvious: not merely the chip, but the time that chip spends waiting. Every system has a hidden queue; eventually, the queue becomes the product. The same constraint appears in handoffs between people, APIs and agents—usually disguised as “just one more status check” on the critical path.

The Lead

The network is becoming part of the computer

Cornelis Networks has raised US$205 million, launched an “Active Compute Fabric,” and announced a Qualcomm collaboration around AI infrastructure. The company says its architecture puts programmable compute in the network, so collective operations and congestion control need not all consume accelerator time. That is a vendor claim, not a benchmark result; still, the bottleneck is credible. Independent analysis describes AI clusters as constrained by synchronization and east-west traffic, leaving expensive accelerators idle while data catches up. The investment thesis is more interesting than another GPU challenger: make the connective tissue do useful work. For anyone pricing an AI feature, that shifts the question from “which model?” to “where does the work wait?”

Why it matters: once a workload spans many machines, the network becomes a first-order lever on latency, throughput and unit cost.
The Takeaway
AI infrastructure is moving from a component market to a flow market. The winning stack will not simply own the best chip; it will minimise dead time between model steps, data movement and the decision that unlocks the next action.
🤖 AI & Tech
Context, not just capability, is becoming the product surface
01
Superhuman has acquired meeting-notetaker Fathom, saying it will connect spoken meetings to its email, calendar, documents and agent workflows. Capture is a dead-end artifact until it can supply context to the next job. The company’s framing is self-interested, but the product lesson holds: decisions, owners and permissions must travel with the transcript, not disappear inside it.
02
OpenAI says its new Data agent can query approved enterprise sources while respecting their table, row and column restrictions. It is a product announcement, not an independent audit. Yet it points to the right architecture: an agent should inherit the organisation’s semantic definitions and entitlements, rather than invent a parallel, ungoverned data universe.
03
OpenAI describes its Habitat storage platform serving more than 70 million requests a second and 1 billion weekly users. The instructive detail is that it pulled storage from a client library into a service, centralising observability, access control and rollouts. Platform teams earn their keep by reducing coordination fan-out.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Coordination tax: the work between the work
Intuition: coordination tax is the useful work your system cannot do because its parts are waiting to agree, exchange state or unlock one another. Think of a relay team: the runners may be world-class, but the race is lost in clumsy baton handovers.

Mechanism:
parallel task → shared dependency → synchronization wait → idle capacity → higher latency and cost
Cornelis is attacking this tax at network level, but the same logic applies to a product team, API workflow or multi-agent system. Parallelism only helps when work can proceed independently. If every branch repeatedly waits for one database, approval or context window, adding workers amplifies the queue.

Try this (20 min): take one workflow with three people, services or agents. Mark each handoff, then time the waiting rather than the doing. Name the one dependency that serialises the whole flow; that is your coordination-tax candidate before you add another model or hire another operator.
🚀 Startup Story
When discovery moves from rankings to answers
Profound says it raised a US$180 million Series D at a US$1.8 billion valuation to help brands appear in AI-generated answers. Its reported revenue tripled in six months and it has more than 1,000 enterprise customers; these are company claims reported by TechCrunch, not audited results. The underlying wager is more durable: if customers ask an assistant rather than search a page of links, the growth surface changes from rank position to inclusion, citation and recommendation. Do not chase answer-engine tricks. Build factual product language, structured documentation and proof a model can retrieve without hallucinating. The answer layer will reward operational clarity before clever copy.
