The Lead
Bessent says a Hormuz deal could land “today or tomorrow” — but nobody's signed anything yet
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC Tuesday the US and Iran are in active talks and “there is a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow” to reopen the Strait of Hormuz — a sharp reversal from Monday, when Tehran flatly denied any negotiations were happening. Iran and Oman separately held what both sides called “positive” talks on a safe shipping corridor through the strait, with Secretary of State Rubio saying the US is involved and progress has been made — though no agreement has been reached. Markets didn't wait for the ink: US stocks hit fresh records Tuesday and oil plunged on the optimism alone (more below).

Why it matters: Monday's flat denial and Tuesday's “deal today or tomorrow” came from the same negotiation — proof this war has always moved faster behind closed doors than either side admits in public.
The Takeaway
A flat denial on Monday and “deal today or tomorrow” on Tuesday aren't a contradiction — they're the same negotiation moving at the speed diplomacy actually moves, dressed differently for two domestic audiences. Whether Wednesday brings a signature or just another news cycle of “progress” is the only test that matters; markets have already voted for the former.

🤖 AI & Tech
Alibaba ships a 2.4-trillion-parameter open model, Anthropic locks up a decade of Norwegian compute, and a third agent-security round lands in a month
Alibaba unveiled Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model it claims rivals OpenAI and Anthropic: 95 billion active parameters via sparse MoE, a 1-million-token context window, open weights shipping next week — the first Max-class Qwen released openly, and a real option for anyone who'd rather self-host than rent API access.

Anthropic signed a $10 billion, six-year compute deal with Volta, a cloud startup founded this year, to build a 133-megawatt Norway data centre on Nvidia's next-gen Vera Rubin chips. Locking up a decade of a brand-new supplier's capacity says a lot about how compute-constrained the whole industry still is.

Horizon3.ai raised $250 million at a valuation north of $2 billion, tripling in a year, to expand its autonomous pentesting platform — NodeZero has now run 300,000+ tests across 6,500 organisations, and it's adding autonomous remediation agents on the defence side too. AI-versus-AI security isn't a pilot anymore.

Broadcom started shipping Jericho4, an Ethernet chip built to link over a million GPUs across separate data centres: 51.2Tbps switching, lossless routing beyond 100km, 40% less power per bit — the plumbing that lets labs pool multiple sites into one training run once a single site runs out of power.

Hush Security raised $30 million, with Akamai joining as a strategic investor, to govern AI agents' identities inside the enterprise — the third agent-security round in a month after Zenity and Onyx (both covered this week), but this one's about credentials, not behaviour. Gartner's reasoning: the average Fortune 500 firm could run 150,000 agents by 2028, up from under 15 a year ago.
🌏 World & Malaysia
Ceuta's toll climbs to 88, a Ukrainian drone hits a Russian beach resort, and Malaysia's 1MDB trial can't trace where Najib's $620 million went
Spain's confirmed Ceuta death toll rose to 88, up from Monday's 72, as roughly 60,000 people breached Morocco's frontier into the enclave in one stretch. The EU is convening talks on uncontrolled crossings; a containment fence is under construction at the breakwater.

A Ukrainian drone strike hit a Russian Black Sea beach resort, killing seven — including three children — and injuring 58 in Gelendzhik. Moscow blamed Kyiv and struck back at Ukraine; Kyiv hasn't confirmed or denied the attack.

Indonesia closed the case on last week's Mutiara Sentosa 2 ferry fire: five dead, everyone else among the 238 aboard rescued or accounted for. The cause off Madura island is still unknown, and regulators now face scrutiny over lax maritime oversight.

Najib's lawyer got a UK fraud investigator to admit he never traced whether the US$620 million Najib “returned” to Tanore Finance flowed back to him, in the RM51.4 billion 1MDB civil trial (as covered Monday). Hearings before the High Court resume today.

The FBM KLCI extended its winning streak to five straight sessions, up 0.4% to 1,732.66 on Tuesday on regional optimism — a calmer, steadier climb than Wall Street's headline-driven sprint to records the same day.

Bursa Malaysia Bhd's own first-half profit jumped, and the exchange operator raised its 2026 IPO target to RM34 billion from RM28 billion after RM26.1 billion was raised across 36 listings in the first half — a vote of confidence in Malaysia's listing pipeline for the rest of the year.

🚀 Startup Story
A Benioff-backed startup is betting AI's real bottleneck was never the model
June emerged from stealth Monday with $20 million in pre-seed funding led by Marc Benioff's Time Ventures, alongside Michael Dell, Aaron Levie and George Kurtz. Founder Efrat Rapoport, an ex-Salesforce exec who previously built and sold a voice-to-text startup to Salesforce, argues enterprise AI isn't failing because models are weak — it's failing because most companies' data and legacy systems are too messy for any model to work against reliably. Her pitch, near-heretical in a market obsessed with model quality: “AI, paradoxically, increases the demand for professional services.”

The lesson for builders: everyone's racing to ship the smartest agent, but most enterprise buyers' real bottleneck is integration — cleaning up the plumbing an agent has to run on. A services layer built around that mess can beat another wrapper on someone else's model.
