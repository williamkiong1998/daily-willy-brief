The Lead
Iran tests the ceasefire pause with a missile attack on a US base in Jordan — and loses
Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps forces fired multiple ballistic missiles at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan around 5:45pm ET Tuesday, in what CENTCOM called an “attempted surprise attack” on American forces. Every missile was intercepted; no casualties were reported. It's Iran's first missile attack on a US base since Trump paused strikes last Friday — as covered since Sunday, that quiet had held for three straight days while Omani mediators worked the phones. Trump had told Axios on Monday the US was in “very deep talks” with Tehran but would return to “very strong military action” if diplomacy failed. Hours earlier, Netanyahu had left the White House after an “ironclad” meeting on Iran's nuclear programme — talks the evening's attack immediately overtook.

Why it matters: a ceasefire that survives exactly as long as neither side tests it isn't a ceasefire — it's a countdown, and Tehran just started the clock.
The Takeaway
The pause that started Sunday was always a pause, not a peace, and Tehran just field-tested the difference — probing Washington's patience with an attack it knew would fail rather than staying quiet. Trump now has to decide whether a swatted-down missile counts as the diplomatic failure he warned would trigger “very strong military action,” or whether intercepted attacks are simply the cost of doing business with a ceasefire this fragile.

🤖 AI & Tech
China ships its first homegrown chipmaking tools, Claude Opus 5 gets a 1M-token window, and the protocol behind agentic AI hits version 1.0
A Shanghai-backed chipmaker just shipped China's first homegrown immersion lithography tools: machines printing 28-nanometre features, pushed to 7nm via multipatterning. Only five units ship this year against ASML's 130, but the psychological damage was instant — the headline that erased Samsung's two decades of good days and tipped the Nasdaq-100 into correction (more below).

Anthropic quietly shipped the model a lot of William's toolchain will run on next: Claude Opus 5 launched last Friday with a 1M-token context window as both default and maximum, state-of-the-art agentic-coding benchmarks, and pricing frozen at Opus 4.8's $5/$25 per million tokens. No smaller variant — Anthropic is betting builders want one very capable model, not a menu.

73% of enterprises now use AI regularly across their business — but only 10% call it core to how they operate, per Publicis Sapient's new 1,550-executive global survey. The gap isn't willingness: 42% say the technology itself is ready but their organisation isn't, still routing agents through workflows built for humans instead of rebuilding around what agents can actually do.

OpenAI's enterprise voice-agent platform is already answering its own support line: Presence, which launched in limited availability last week, resolves 75% of OpenAI's own inbound customer calls without a human, the company says. A rare case of a lab eating its own dog food before selling it — worth watching if you're building anything customer-facing.

The Model Context Protocol just left beta: the final 1.0 spec landed Tuesday with Tasks and MCP Apps extensions, formalising how agents discover and call external tools — the same protocol stitching together the tools running underneath this newsletter. LangGraph already treats MCP tools as first-class nodes; expect every agent framework to follow.

📈 Markets
Samsung's worst day in two decades drags the Nasdaq-100 into correction, and the Fed decides this afternoon
The S&P 500 closed Tuesday at 7,428.78 (+0.21%) and the Dow jumped 1.03% to 52,747.32 on a rotation out of chips, while the Nasdaq Composite slipped for a fifth straight session to 24,876.91 (-0.22%) as the Nasdaq-100 fell into correction territory, down more than 10% from its June record. Cashflow Lens: Qualcomm, a 21-year dividend grower, got caught in the same downdraft — down 4.2% Tuesday, pushing its yield to about 2.3% against a five-year average near 2.1% (h/t @the_prosperityplan for the discount-on-fear framing). Safety check: payout ratio in the mid-30s on both earnings and free cash flow — nothing about today's chip panic touches Qualcomm's phone-royalty engine. Reads as a discount, not a warning.

🚀 Startup Story
SpaceX's $60 billion answer to “build a moat or get bought”
Six weeks after taking Cursor off the market for $60 billion in stock — the largest venture-backed startup acquisition ever, at roughly 15x revenue — the deal is still pending approval, and it's aging into the cleanest lesson in this year's AI-wrapper cull. Cursor wasn't a thin wrapper; it built the deepest IDE workflow in its category, the kind of proprietary moat survivors like ElevenLabs (voice models) and Perplexity (search index) also hold. It still couldn't stay independent once labs started shipping comparable coding agents natively. The steal for anyone building on a frontier model this week: a real moat doesn't guarantee independence, it just guarantees you get bought instead of copied — decide which outcome you're building toward.
