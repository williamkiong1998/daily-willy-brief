The Lead

The model is not the moat. The map is.

Atlassian argues that context—not another model upgrade—is the durable unlock for enterprise agents. Its point is more practical than philosophical: an agent needs the current work, people, decisions, code, documents and permissions connected in a form it can traverse. A pile of connected sources is not enough. The useful layer tells the system that yesterday’s chat reverses last week’s decision, that “Project Titan” has three meanings, and that a policy applies only to one team. That turns retrieval from a scavenger hunt into operating knowledge. Atlassian calls the practice context engineering; its useful tests are completeness, connectedness and relevance. Its vendor claims deserve normal scrutiny, but the design principle travels.

Why it matters: capability is becoming easier to buy. The defensible work is making your organisation legible, current and permission-aware enough for capability to act well.
The Takeaway
Context is product infrastructure, not prompt garnish. The teams that win will make the right facts, boundaries and decisions easy to retrieve—and make the wrong ones hard to use—before an agent begins a task.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Context contracts: make the map inspectable
Intuition: a context contract is the legend on a map. It says which sources count, how fresh they must be, who owns them and when the traveller must stop and ask. Without it, retrieval is like letting a sharp new consultant loose in a filing room: they may find an answer, but not necessarily the current or permitted one.

Mechanism:
task → scoped sources + freshness rule → ranked evidence → permission check → answer or escalation
Store provenance with each returned fact: source, owner, timestamp, scope and confidence. Define the conflict rule before a model encounters one—for example, the latest approved decision overrides a draft, while a policy document overrides a meeting note. That makes the failure visible and recoverable rather than merely eloquent.

Try this (20 min): choose one recurring decision, such as a client status update. Write a five-line contract: question, allowed sources, freshness window, tie-breaker when sources disagree, and person who approves an external send. Then ask an agent to answer only inside that contract and inspect what it refuses.
🔧 Product Craft
Make the quality bar runnable
Atlassian says 89% of executives see AI speeding work, while only 6% can point to organisation-wide ROI. That gap is a product-craft problem. Faster output makes a vague quality bar more expensive, not less. Borrow the design-system pattern: express the bar as things a team and an agent can use—acceptance criteria, examples, prohibited patterns, source-of-truth data and a named final decision. A PRD that only describes intent forces every contributor to reconstruct the standard. A runnable bar lets people prototype quickly while keeping discovery, evidence and judgement close to the work. The unit of leverage is not “more drafts”; it is a shorter loop from evidence to a decision you can defend.
