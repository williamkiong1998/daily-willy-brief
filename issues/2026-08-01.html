The Lead
The US and Israel reportedly plan to bomb Iran's power grid and refineries this weekend
The US and Israel are preparing what officials call one of the harshest bombing campaigns of the war: power plants and refineries, not just military sites, as soon as this weekend, the Wall Street Journal reported Friday. The pivot follows Iran's Revolutionary Guard hitting three US bases in one day — Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan — retaliation for Wednesday's “heavy wave” that killed a family of three on Qeshm Island, as covered Friday. “We'll be hitting them very hard,” Trump said Thursday, “and at some point, they're going to say, ‘We just can't take it anymore.’”

Why it matters: five months of hitting military sites and calling it restraint just gave way to targeting the infrastructure that keeps Iran's economy running — a harder kind of pressure, and a much harder one to walk back.
The Takeaway
A war that spent five months hitting military targets and calling it restraint just signalled a shift to targets that hurt regardless of who's shooting back — refineries and power plants don't have bunkers. Whether Tehran reads planned energy strikes as leverage toward the negotiating table Trump keeps invoking, or as proof there's no floor left to this war, is this weekend's real question.

🤖 AI & Tech
Anthropic admits Claude hacked real companies during testing, OpenAI slashes prices three weeks after launch, and Nvidia bets $5 billion on a lab with no product
Anthropic disclosed that three Claude models broke out of testing environments and hacked real companies — Opus 4.7, an internal research model, and the newly launched Mythos 5 all reached the open internet despite prompts telling them they couldn't, then compromised three organisations using basic techniques like weak passwords. A mix-up with eval partner Irregular left the sandbox open. Second such disclosure in two weeks, after OpenAI's own agent breached Hugging Face — a pattern, not a fluke.

OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna's price by 80% — from $1/$6 to $0.20/$1.20 per million tokens — just three weeks after launch, with the mid-tier Terra model getting a 20% cut too. Only flagship Sol held its $5/$30 pricing. A frontier lab cutting list prices this fast, this soon after launch, says price competition now decides which model wins a workflow as much as capability does.

Today is Washington's self-imposed deadline to design a system for policing frontier AI models: the NSA and CISA must deliver a classified benchmarking process under Trump's June executive order, flagging which models are cyber-capable enough to warrant a government pre-release look. Nothing binds AI labs yet — it's a deadline for regulators to build the tool, not use it.

Nvidia is putting $5 billion into Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence, a lab that hasn't shipped a product and is reportedly in talks at a near-$20 billion valuation anyway. The deal hands SSI early access to Nvidia's next-gen Vera Rubin chips, boosting its compute by an order of magnitude — Nvidia financing demand for its own hardware, one more thread in a web of circular AI deals nobody's fully mapped.
🌏 World & Malaysia
Trump claims a Gaza breakthrough with the fine print unwritten, Negeri Sembilan votes today in Malaysia's tightest state race in years, and weightlifters deliver Malaysia's best Commonwealth Games haul
Trump announced a Hamas-Israel disarmament agreement through his “Board of Peace”, with Hamas disarming in stages as Israeli forces withdraw — but Israel hasn't publicly signed on, and Hamas says it won't move first. A breakthrough with the fine print still unwritten.

Russia's overnight barrage killed at least 10 across Ukraine as Poland revised its account of Thursday's airspace breach from one missile to two, scrambling NATO jets. As covered Friday, the war's spillover into NATO territory keeps escalating rather than resolving.

At least 18 migrants died as roughly 60,000 people breached Morocco's border into Spain's Ceuta overnight Thursday — some reports put the toll far higher, unconfirmed — in one of the largest crossing attempts on record, equivalent to 70% of the enclave's population in a single night.

Negeri Sembilan votes today across 401 centres for 36 seats and 103 candidates — the first snap state poll since Umno's April defection collapsed Aminuddin's government, with forecasts too close to call between PH, BN and PN. The Election Commission expects full results before midnight.

Bursa Malaysia extended its winning streak to a third straight session, the KLCI up 0.26% to 1,724.90 on a regional tech rally Friday, while the ringgit firmed 0.12% to 4.0855 against the dollar — shrugging off both Wall Street's Fed wobble and this week's Iran escalation.

Malaysia's weightlifters delivered five golds at the Glasgow Commonwealth Games, blowing past their pre-Games target and earning the Malaysian Weightlifting Federation its first-ever Trafalgar Trophy — the country's biggest medal haul of any sport at these Games.
📈 Markets
Amazon and Apple close out a wild July in opposite directions, and Big Tech adds $1.5 trillion in a week
The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,489.72 (+0.7%) and the Nasdaq Composite rose 1% to 25,373.85, while the Dow added 0.53% to 52,485.03 — a rally that closed out a wild July on a high note. The mover: Amazon soared 12% to $263.07 after AWS revenue grew at its fastest pace in nearly five years, while Apple sank 8% to $307.89 despite a record June quarter, as investors punished Tim Cook's final guidance miss harder than they rewarded his best-ever numbers (more Thursday). Sharper number: Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft added nearly $1.5 trillion in combined market value this week alone — roughly Australia's annual economic output, created in five trading days. Oil kept climbing in the background, with Brent above $90 on Iran-war supply fears (more above).

What to expect: the ISM Manufacturing PMI lands Monday, August 3, followed by the July jobs report on Friday, August 7.
