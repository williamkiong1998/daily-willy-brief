The price of money has re-entered the room. Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole address makes the constraint explicit just as Malaysia is building capacity for investment, data centres and a more resilient grid. Today’s brief follows the chain from the discount rate to the operating choices it changes.
The Lead
The Fed has put the cost of capital back in the product brief.
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh says inflation remains above the Fed’s 2% target: PCE inflation is 3.7% over 12 months and 4.1% over six. His Jackson Hole speech did not pre-commit to a move, but it stressed the FOMC’s readiness to act and described financial conditions as not broadly restrictive. That changes the planning context for every business with long-dated cash flows. The same speech says equipment and intangible-investment growth has been about 9% over four quarters, with more than half of this year’s growth likely tied to AI buildout. Investment is still happening; the hurdle rate has simply become harder to ignore.

Why it matters: a feature roadmap can look unchanged while its economics change underneath it. Higher financing costs raise the premium on fast payback, reliable revenue and infrastructure that earns its keep.
The Takeaway
Capital is not an abstract macro variable. It is the quiet editor of product ambition: it decides which experiments can wait, which platform spend needs proof, and which growth claims must turn into cash before the next planning cycle.

🌏 World & Malaysia
The next constraints are arriving through trade, capital and wires
AP reports the White House is considering a 7.5% tariff on Chinese goods. The claim rests on three unnamed people and is not final; Malaysia is among economies in related U.S. trade investigations. For operators, this is a reminder that supply-chain pricing can move before an announced rule exists.
New U.S.-Canada tariffs cover about US$20 billion of Canadian exports to the United States, according to AP. Canada’s counter-tariffs would begin 8 September. Whether this de-escalates or not, trade policy is again a direct input into cost, inventory and contract design.
MIDA says Malaysia approved RM218.5 billion of investment across 2,746 projects in H1. That is approvals, not yet delivered investment; the projects are expected to create 99,030 jobs. The execution test is converting commitments into connected sites, skilled teams and customers.
TNB has started work on a 500kV National Grid Backbone scheduled for completion by 2030. Bernama reports roughly 500km of planned line, designed to support renewables, AI industries and data centres. Compute capacity is only as real as the energy system behind it.
📈 Markets
The market heard the rate message; small caps heard it louder
Friday’s close: the S&P 500 fell 0.2% to 7,711.76, while the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.5% to 26,402.42. The notable mover was the Russell 2000, down 1.4%. That is consistent with a simple mechanism, not a forecast: smaller companies typically have less room to absorb expensive funding, so a more credible higher-rate path bites earlier. The broader index reaction was restrained, while the week still left the S&P up 0.5% and the Nasdaq up 0.8%. The market is repricing a hurdle rate, not declaring a recession.

What to expect: Canada’s announced counter-tariffs are scheduled to take effect on 8 September; U.S. investors will also watch the next labour and inflation releases for evidence against the Fed’s policy test. The Cashflow Lens did not fire: this was not a broad fear-driven dividend selloff.
A modest headline decline concealed a sharper move in smaller, more funding-sensitive companies.
🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Cost of capital: the invisible acceptance criterion
Intuition: cost of capital is the price a business pays for getting tomorrow’s money today. Think of it as the gravity in a product plan: a far-off payoff can still be valuable, but it needs more lift than an immediate one. When rates rise, the same revenue forecast is worth less in today’s decision.

Mechanism:
future cash flow → discount rate → present value → investment hurdle → roadmap and pricing choice
Do not use it to kill every long bet. Use it to separate an assumption from a funded commitment. The question becomes: what evidence would let this AI feature clear its hurdle—retention, paid conversion, lower service cost or a defensible data advantage? That makes the rate environment a design constraint, not a finance-team weather report.

Try this (20 min): choose one roadmap item that will cost money now and is meant to pay back later. Write its first measurable cash consequence, the time to reach it, and the evidence that would make you fund the next tranche. You have turned a feature bet into an investable hypothesis.
