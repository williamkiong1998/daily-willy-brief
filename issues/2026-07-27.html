The Lead
A fragile Iran pause holds for a second straight day, and Oman brokers progress on a Hormuz deal
As covered Sunday, when the US paused strikes on Iran for the first time in 13 nights, that quiet has now held for a second straight day, after Trump simply stopped signing off on the nightly Pentagon strike plans he'd approved for two weeks. Iran signaled it would reciprocate, with a source telling US News the country will halt its own attacks “as long as the United States maintains” the pause. In Tehran, Omani mediators spent the weekend on technical talks that made “progress” on letting Iran manage Strait of Hormuz vessel transit with fewer restrictions, extending the fee-waiver framework agreed in June. Brent crude, which broke $100 last Thursday, extended its slide past $92 by Monday morning, down more than 5%, while Nasdaq 100 futures jumped 1.2% and Dow futures pushed above the 52,000 mark.

Why it matters: a truce built on both sides simultaneously choosing not to strike is a pause, not a peace — markets are pricing in a ceasefire that still hasn't been put on paper.
The Takeaway
Two full days without a single strike, plus real progress on a Hormuz framework, is the strongest de-escalation signal since this phase of the war reignited — and for the first time, oil and equities are both pricing it as durable rather than hopeful. But Iran's own condition, reciprocity “as long as” the pause holds, is exactly the kind of clause that snaps the moment either side reads the other's intentions wrong.

🤖 AI & Tech
Moonshot open-sources the biggest model ever built, Claude's share links leak into Google, and enterprises admit AI still isn't paying for itself
Moonshot's Kimi K3 goes fully open today, weights and all: 2.8 trillion parameters on Hugging Face under a modified MIT license, the largest open-weight release in history. As covered Sunday, when Jensen Huang used his first tweet to defend open models, this is the release that argument was about — frontier coding, self-hostable for zero per-token cost.

Claude's public share links turned into a Google-search privacy leak: hundreds of shared conversations, some containing API keys and CVs, were discoverable through plain Google searches after users posted the links elsewhere online. Anthropic says it blocks crawlers from share pages and found no breach — but roughly 600 conversations were indexed before disappearing.

Grok just quietly became a Google Docs add-on: SpaceXAI shipped a free Grok extension for Docs, Sheets and Slides Friday, weeks after doing the same for Microsoft Office. Every frontier lab is racing to live inside the tools people already have open, not pull them into a new one.

A 639-company survey just punctured the AI-ROI narrative: Domino Data Lab's fifth annual report found 57% of enterprises still can't get AI returns to outpace spend, unchanged since 2025, even as 93% report better production capability. The gap is last-mile — demo-ready models, not yet in business users' hands.
🌏 World & Malaysia
Kyiv takes another hit, Sepang gets its F1 race back, and Negeri Sembilan's polls and odds disagree
Russia hit Kyiv with ballistic missiles again overnight Sunday: a residential building was struck in a wider barrage — 7 missiles, 136 drones nationwide — that killed at least seven civilians and injured 58. Zelensky's warning of a bigger strike still coming hasn't arrived, but hasn't gone away either.

Europe's wildfires are closing in on 400,000 evacuated: fires near Madrid threaten to merge, one has come within nine miles of Bordeaux, and Macron activated the EU's civil protection mechanism for aircraft and crews from four countries. No rain is forecast this week.

Israel is preparing its biggest West Bank operation in years: after a shootout near a Palestinian village left four Palestinians and two Israeli soldiers dead Friday, troops raided a Nablus hospital to capture two suspects and announced an “extensive” counterterror campaign is coming.

Malaysia just got its Formula 1 race back: as teased Sunday, F1, the FIA, and the Bahrain and Malaysian governments confirmed Sepang will host the rescheduled Bahrain Grand Prix on October 4 — its first race since 2017, in a triple-header with Azerbaijan and Singapore.

Suhakam is pushing back on a ruling that could leave children stateless: after the Court of Appeal ruled Malaysian fathers can't automatically pass citizenship to children born out of wedlock, the human rights commission urged Putrajaya to use executive discretion instead.

Negeri Sembilan's polls and its odds are telling different stories: a Vodus survey has Pakatan leading the popular vote (42% to BN's 35%) and projected seats (17 to 15), yet prediction markets favour BN at 66% — its support is simply distributed more efficiently ahead of August 1.
📈 Markets
Airlines catch a break, oil gives back its war premium, and the real test starts with the Fed on Wednesday
Friday's close still stands ahead of the US open: the S&P 500 finished at 7,411.98 (+0.05%) and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.64% to 24,975.82. The mover is underneath the indices: as Brent crude extended its retreat from Thursday's break above $100 to below $92 Monday morning — down more than 5% — airline and cruise stocks, which spent two weeks sinking on war-driven fuel-cost fear, are set to open firmer, while the energy names that rallied on that same fear face the mirror move. Sharper number: Nasdaq 100 futures are up 1.2% and Dow futures have pushed back above 52,000, erasing most of Friday's chip-driven weakness in a single premarket session.

What to expect: the Fed's rate decision lands Wednesday, July 29 at 2pm ET — a fifth straight hold at 3.50–3.75% is priced at high odds under Chair Kevin Warsh — with Microsoft and Meta reporting that same evening and Apple and Amazon following Thursday, July 30.
