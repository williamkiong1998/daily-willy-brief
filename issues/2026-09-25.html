The Lead

A truce is a deadline, not a strategy

The United States and China have extended their trade truce from 10 November to 10 January 2027, according to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. That keeps tariff escalation paused, but it is a two-month bridge—not a settled commercial architecture. The same report says China is committed to buy 25 million tonnes of US soybeans annually and US$17 billion of other farm and food products; Bessent said progress is uneven on the latter. For a builder, the lesson is less geopolitical than operational: a policy reprieve has a clock, a named set of dependencies and a next decision date. Treat it as an input to procurement, pricing and capacity plans, not permission to stop modelling the downside.

Why it matters: the firms that benefit from temporary stability will be the ones that convert it into options before the next expiry turns uncertainty back into a cost.
The Takeaway
Stability is most useful when it has an owner and an expiry. The next 107 days should produce a decision log: which dependency is tolerated until January, which needs a substitute, and what evidence would justify renewing the bet.

🧠 Builder’s Notebook
Commitment horizons: make uncertainty legible before it becomes urgent
Intuition: a commitment horizon is the date until which a decision remains safe enough to leave alone. Think of a ferry timetable: you do not need to rebuild the harbour every morning, but you do need to know the last crossing, the alternative route and who decides when weather closes the channel.

Mechanism:
dependency → fixed commitment → review horizon → exit option → continuity or change
A trade truce, cloud contract, model API commitment or regulatory exemption is not binary. Give each one an expiry, an owner, a lead time to switch and a trigger that forces review. The goal is not to predict disruption; it is to avoid discovering a critical dependency only when it becomes expensive to replace.

Try this (20 min): open one live product roadmap or vendor contract. Make a four-column list: dependency, next binding date, switching lead time, evidence needed to renew. Circle any row where the lead time is longer than the time remaining. That row deserves a contingency experiment this week.
🚀 Business Story
Google is testing whether energy geography can be redesigned
Google’s Project Suncatcher will launch a prototype satellite to test how its AI hardware behaves under radiation, vibration and vacuum-cooling constraints. This is a research moonshot, not an imminent data-centre alternative; Google itself frames the work as an early test. The business lesson is more grounded than the orbit: when a system’s bottleneck is power, a company can either optimise within today’s geography or ask whether the geography itself is the constraint worth changing. Most teams should choose the first path. But the strategic habit is valuable: write down the physical constraint beneath your product cost curve, then distinguish an engineering problem from an inherited assumption.
